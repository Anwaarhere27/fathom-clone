## Google Calendar OAuth

**What it buys us.** Fathom's onboarding hinges on it: connect calendar → it lists your upcoming meetings → each one gets a "record this" toggle → the bot shows up when the meeting starts. Without a calendar the app has no concept of a *future* meeting, only past ones. That's the flow we're replicating.

**Two ways to do it, and they're meaningfully different:**

1. **Supabase Auth's Google provider with extra scopes.** You get Google sign-in and calendar access in one consent. Cheapest to build — maybe 30 minutes. But Supabase doesn't persist the provider tokens; they live in the session and vanish on expiry. And it welds "sign in" to "connect calendar," which is *not* how Fathom works.
2. **Our own OAuth routes** — `/api/google/connect` and `/api/google/callback` — storing the refresh token in a `google_connections` table. ~1.5–2 hours. This gives us calendar connect as a distinct step after signup, which matches Fathom's IA and gives you a genuine "here's the connect screen" beat in the walkthrough.

I'd build **#2**. The extra hour buys the product moment that actually gets demoed.

**Setup, when you're ready** (console.cloud.google.com):

1. New project, name it `fathom-clone`.
2. **APIs & Services → Library** → search "Google Calendar API" → **Enable**.
3. **Google Auth Platform → Branding**: app name, your email as support contact and developer contact.
4. **Audience**: set to **External**, and under **Test users** add your own Gmail (`anwaar.muhammad11@gmail.com`).
5. **Data access → Add scopes**: `.../auth/calendar.readonly` and `.../auth/calendar.events.readonly`.
6. **Credentials → Create Credentials → OAuth client ID → Web application**:
   - Authorized JavaScript origins: `http://localhost:3000`
   - Authorized redirect URIs: `http://localhost:3000/api/google/callback`
   - We'll add the Vercel URLs once that domain exists.
7. Copy the **Client ID** and **Client Secret** back here.
